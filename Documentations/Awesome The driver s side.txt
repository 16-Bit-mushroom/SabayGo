Awesome. The driver's side is all about efficiency and rapid decision-making. Since we want this to be bulletproof for your panel, we need this flow to feel incredibly responsive.

Just like we did for the commuter, the absolute best way to handle this is by building a dedicated `DriverViewModel` with a strict state machine. This ensures the driver's UI never gets confused about what step of the job they are currently on.

### **The Driver State Machine**

Here is the logical lifecycle of a driver's shift.

* **`offline`**: The default state. The map is disabled or simplified, and no requests come in.
* **`online`**: The driver goes "on duty." The app actively listens to the backend algorithm for nearby nodes.
* **`requestReceived`**: A high-priority overlay appears with the commuter's details, pickup node, drop-off node, and estimated fare. The driver can Accept or Decline.
* **`headingToPickup`**: The driver accepted the ride and the map switches to navigation mode.
* **`arrivedAtPickup`**: The driver has arrived at the starting node and is waiting for the commuter to enter the vehicle.
* **`inRide`**: The commuter is secured, and the driver is navigating to the drop-off node. (This is where the purple "Slide to Drop-off" button will live).
* **`completed`**: The trip finishes, and the driver sees their specific summary—focused on fare earnings, service fees, and ratings.
